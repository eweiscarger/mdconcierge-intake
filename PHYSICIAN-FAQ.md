## Who is who

Three separate parties, and the separation is the point.

- **Excel Pharmacy** is the pharmacy. It dispenses and ships.
- **MDRx** is the billing and collections solution for work comp pharmacy. It buys nothing from the
  pharmacy and owns none of it. **MDRx does not own Excel.** They are in-network partners.
- **The physician or practice entity** is the third party, participating through its own LLC or the
  practice entity.

**Eric's position: the most compliant programs have three parties.** Dispensing, billing and
participation sit with three parties that do not own one another, at arm's length and at fair market
value. That structure is a feature, not an accident of how the vendors happen to be arranged.

**In the copy it is one program.** Eric, when this was written up as three parties to be spelled out
in the emails: *"they are one thing, don't get it confused."*

The three-party separation is how the arrangement is structured and why it holds up. It is **not** a
thing to explain to a physician in an outreach email. He is being offered one program that works, not
an org chart. Turning the copy into a corporate structure lesson makes a simple offer sound
complicated and invites a question he was not asking.

**If you use MDRx, you use Excel. Plain and simple.** There is no pharmacy to choose and no decision
for the physician to make about it. The two come together as one program.

So: present it as one thing. MDRx is named as the partner running it, the pharmacy stays unnamed, and
the entity separation is background that surfaces only if someone asks, or in the compliance material
where it belongs.

Naming rules are unchanged and live in `COPY-RULES.md`: **MDRx is named** in copy, it is credibility
rather than a jolt to manage. **Excel is not named**; the phrasing is "an independent, licensed
mail-order pharmacy" or "one of our in-network pharmacies."

## 1. What does it pay?

**65 percent, minus the cost of the medication, dispensing and shipping.**

That is the whole formula. It is not a range and it is not "up to."

**Publishing it is settled: there is no issue stating it.** Eric: *"we are not hiding anything
here."* It is already stated in full on `mdrx-brief.html`, including that the claim is acquired at
fair market value plus dispense fee and shipping, that MDRx bills the payer, and that MDRx bears the
billing and collection risk.

**The live problem is reachability, not policy.** `mdrx-brief.html` is linked only from
`admin-v2.html` and from `go.html` itself. No physician-facing path reaches it. Touch 1 sends
`to=program`, which resolves to `brief.html`, and `brief.html` says only "shares in the revenue" and
never links onward to the MDRx brief. A physician who does exactly what we want, which is educate
himself, lands on the vague version and stops one page short of the answer.

## 2. What do I have to sign?

**A Claims Purchase and Management Services Agreement. That is it.**

One agreement. Not a stack. Worth stating plainly, because "the agreements go out for signature" on
the brief page implies more paperwork than actually exists and invites him to imagine the worst.

## 3. How do I hold it?

Three cases, and the answer differs by each:

- **An individual physician participating on his own** uses an **LLC**.
- **A whole practice participating** can run it **through the practice entity**.
- **A hospital-employed physician** depends on **what his employment contract permits**. Those
  physicians typically use an **LLC**.

## 4. What about my malpractice carrier, or my hospital?

**Outside Eric's scope, and it stays outside.** He does not advise on it and will not.

On the hospital specifically, the honest position is:

- If the physician's contract does not prohibit it, participation is **at the hospital's discretion**.
- The physician can also **disclose it and participate** if the hospital is agreeable.
- **That is the physician's decision to make with his employer, not ours to characterise.**

This is a boundary, not a gap. Do not soften it into reassurance and do not let any agent write a
sentence that sounds like advice about a malpractice carrier or an employment contract.

## 5. What happens when the claim closes?

**Medications are no longer covered under workers' compensation once the claim closes.** From that
point the patient pays through personal insurance.

This is a genuine limitation and it belongs stated plainly alongside the other honest limits, not
buried. The decision page already carries a section called "The honest limits" and this is the
natural place for it.

## What was already right and stays

From `decision.html`, and it is the best honest-limits language on the site:

- The eight designated services stay restricted.
- **Workers' comp and auto claims only. Never Medicare, Medicaid, or any federal claim.**
- The Court noted that if the legislature ever wants to add prescription drugs to the ban, it would
  have to do so explicitly.

That second line is the real limitation a physician wants early, and it was already written and
approved before any of this conversation.

## 6. What if the legislature adds prescription drugs to the ban later?

The decision page raises this possibility, so a physician will ask it. Eric's answer, 2026-08-26:

**The Claims Purchase and Management Services Agreement would be paused. The medication service to
patients continues exactly as it is. The economics to the provider stop. The patient sees no
interruption in their medication.**

Note for anyone writing this: the answer is **patient first, physician second**, and that order is
the answer, not a presentation choice. The thing that stops is the money. The thing that continues is
the care. Do not invert it to lead with what the physician loses.

## 8. Two rules that govern the cold sequence

Eric, 2026-08-26.

### Never cold-outreach a practice administrator

Administrators are approached **only through an introduction.** Cold outreach to an administrator
clouds the situation and can kill the physician outright: if she says no, there is no way to go
around her without damaging the relationship, and the practice is closed before a physician ever saw
it.

This settles the question of whether administrators get their own cold sequence. **They do not.** The
cadence writes to physicians. Administrators enter warm, by introduction, and are handled by Eric.

Note the asymmetry, because it is easy to misread the reply data: administrators are all over the
live pipeline and do most of the work moving a deal through. That is because they arrived warm, not
because they were prospected.

### Never offer to show a physician what he would earn

Saying we will put his numbers in and show him what he would make is **inducement**, because it ties
economics to his prescribing. This is the existing doctrine rule, that we never tie economics to
prescribing, applied to an offer rather than a sentence.

**Wrong:** "Tell me what you prescribe most and I will show you what it looks like against your own
prescribing."

**Right, and Eric's own wording:** "We can customize your formulary based on your current
prescribing, so you can see what it looks like for you or for your practice."

The difference is what is being customized. We tailor **the formulary** to what a practice already
prescribes, which is information about program fit. We do not model **his earnings** as a prompt to
participate.

**When a prospect asks first, that is different.** Two administrators have asked, unprompted, for
cost and reimbursement figures on their own formulary. Answering a question that was put to us is not
the same as offering an earnings projection to open a door.

## 9. The formulary ladder

Eric, 2026-08-26. This is how formulary material is released, and it exists because MDRx does not
want cost information circulating and Eric does not want to be the reason it does.

### Step one, on request. The formulary without cost.

A prospect can ask for the formulary and receive it **with no cost information in it.** Nothing
sensitive leaves, so this can be offered freely and is a good low-friction ask in a cold message: it
is concrete, genuinely useful, and answerable in one word.

### Step two, gated. The interactive tool.

There is an interactive tool that lets a practice select and customize its own formulary, so they can
see what it looks like for them or for their practice. **It is not emailed cold.** Eric walks through
it with them first, on a call or a Zoom, and that step is the first real signal of buying intent.

**After the walkthrough he sends it to them**, so they can use it themselves. This matters to how it
is described: the walkthrough is a gate, not a ceiling. Nothing is being dangled and taken away.

Frame it as how the tool is handed over, which is true, rather than as a toll. A physician can smell
a toll.

### What must never happen

- No cost or reimbursement figures in anything sent unprompted.
- Never describe the tool as showing what he would earn. It customizes **the formulary**, which is
  program fit. See section 8.
- The pharmacy is still never named in copy, whatever the document itself is branded.
