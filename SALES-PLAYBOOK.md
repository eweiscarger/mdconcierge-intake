# Marketing playbook — work comp pharmacy

**Scope: the MDRx workers' compensation pharmacy program, sold to physicians.** MDconcierge runs
several service lines (referral management and medical coordination, mail-order pharmacy, single-use
injection kits, ancillary coordination) and they are not sold the same way to the same people. This
playbook covers pharmacy only. The other lines get their own, and nothing here should be lifted into
an attorney or ancillary email without being rewritten for that audience.

**Status: NOT IMPLEMENTED. Nothing in this file is in force.**
The cadence, `next-move`, the inbox drafters and the copywriter subagent are untouched and still run
Eric's approved copy. This is a document we are building together and reviewing before any of it
changes a single live email. `COPY-RULES.md` remains the binding document and outranks this file
wherever the two differ.

Everything is sourced. Where a claim comes from research rather than opinion, the study is named.

---

## 0. Who we are writing to

A physician who **already writes these prescriptions.** He reads us between patients. He has been
sold to by device reps his entire career and can smell a pitch in one line. He is not looking for a
vendor and he did not ask us to write.

Nothing in this file works if we forget that the message is about **his** prescriptions and **his**
patients, and never about our program.

---

## 1. The spine: where he is, not where we wish he was

Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966), built the **five stages of awareness**. It is the
oldest idea in this file and the most useful, because it tells you what a message is allowed to do
before it has earned the right to do more.
([awareness and sophistication](https://www.kingeshop.com/en/customer-awareness-sophistication-eugene-schwartz),
[five stages](https://betweenthelinescopy.com/blog/stages-of-awareness/))

| Stage | What copy must do |
|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Name a problem he has not recognized |
| **Problem aware** | Validate the pain, show a fix exists |
| **Solution aware** | Compare approaches, introduce the product |
| **Product aware** | Prove superiority with evidence |
| **Most aware** | Present the offer, remove friction |

### What that means for our list

| Stage | The physician in this stage | What he needs from us |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Writes work comp scripts, has never thought about where the revenue goes. **Most of our list.** | The 700 Pharmacy decision, as news about his own world. Nothing else. |
| **Problem aware** | Knows the money goes elsewhere, assumes nothing can be done, or assumes it is not allowed | The holding: the anti-referral statute does not reach prescription drugs. His objection answered before he raises it. |
| **Solution aware** | Knows physician-owned arrangements exist, unsure of the legality or the work | How little changes. Same prescriptions, the pharmacy changes in the EHR. Mail order only. |
| **Product aware** | Knows MDRx, has not committed | Proof. Hundreds of physicians participating. No denials. Overnight to the patient. |
| **Most aware** | Ready | The agreement and the first step. Remove friction. |

**The rule that follows: never write a message more than one stage ahead of him.**

Our recurring failure is writing **Product-aware copy to an Unaware physician.** The billing,
collections and remittance paragraph is stage-four content. It is true, it is well written, and on a
first touch it arrives before the man has agreed there is anything to solve.

Schwartz's own summary of the whole craft: you do not create desire, you **channel desire that already
exists.** The desire is already there. He would rather keep the revenue his own prescriptions generate
than hand it to a pharmacy benefit manager with no role in the treatment. We do not have to
manufacture that. We have to connect it to something he did not know had changed.

## 2. Market sophistication: we are not first

Schwartz's second framework. Sophistication is not about the buyer, it is about **the market**, and it
measures how many times these people have already heard a claim like ours.

| Level | The market | What wins |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Has never heard the claim | State the claim plainly |
| 2 | Has heard it | A bigger, more specific claim |
| 3 | Has heard the bigger claim | A **unique mechanism** |
| 4 | Sick of claims | **Proof** |
| 5 | Claims exhausted | **Identity and belonging** |

**Physicians are at level four, minimum.** Every one of them has been offered a revenue opportunity by
somebody. A plain claim is invisible. A bigger claim is worse than invisible, it is a tell.

Which is why the ruling matters so much. **700 Pharmacy is our unique mechanism.** It is the answer to
"why is this different from the last six of these I deleted," and it is the one thing we hold that no
competitor can copy, because it is not a claim at all. It is a decision of the Pennsylvania Supreme
Court, argued and won by a named attorney who wrote the summary himself.

At level four we also need proof rather than adjectives: hundreds of physicians participating, no
denials, overnight delivery. At level five, identity: **doctors like him are already doing this.**

## 3. The competitor is doing nothing

April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*. Positioning starts by listing the **competitive alternatives**, and
that list includes **what he would do if we did not exist**, which is usually **nothing at all.**
*"Customers evaluate you against their current reality."*
([the 10 steps](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-obviously-awesome/))

We are not competing with another pharmacy program. We are competing with **Tuesday**, where he keeps
writing scripts exactly as he does now, at zero effort and zero risk to himself.

That changes what a touch has to accomplish. Being attractive is not enough. It has to be worth the
cost of switching away from a status quo that is not hurting him today.

**Test for any touch:** does this beat doing nothing, from his chair, this week? If it only beats a
competitor, it beats nobody, because there is no competitor in the room.

## 4. The posture: help, do not sell

**Jay Baer, *Youtility*.** *"Marketing so useful, people would pay for it."* And the line that should
govern first contact: *"If you sell something, you make a customer today. If you genuinely help
someone, you create a customer for life."*

The hard part Baer names is **intentional restraint**: deliberately promoting **less** at the point of
contact, to build trust capital that is redeemed later.
([Youtility](https://www.convinceandconvert.com/digital-marketing/youtility-the-marketing-strategy-for-the-age-of-information-overload/))

**Seth Godin, *This Is Marketing*.** *"Marketing is the generous act of helping someone solve their
problem."* Two ideas worth taking whole
([notes](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/this-is-marketing-seth-godin)):

- **The smallest viable market.** *"The smallest number of people you need to delight for them to tell
  others."* Pennsylvania work comp physicians is a smallest viable market, and treating it as small is
  a strength, not a limitation.
- **"People like us do things like this."** His real question is not "does this pay." It is **"do
  doctors like me do this?"** Godin: *"We sell feelings, status, and connection, not tasks or stuff."*

**What this means concretely.** The Siegel write-up is useful to a work comp physician whether or not
he ever speaks to us. That is Youtility. It is also why it works: we gave first, and we gave something
he would have wanted anyway.

## 4.1 Short body, deep shelf

**The email is a doorway. The reading behind it does the persuading.**

A body that fits a phone screen, and behind it as much material as a physician wants. This corrects
an instinct that has to be named because it was wrong twice in this file's history: that links are
clutter and the resource list at the bottom of touch 1 is weight the email does not need.

It is not weight. It is the engine.

Eric: *"when they read about it, I pitch less, they educate themselves. That's the beauty of this
whole thing."*

Two reasons it works, and both are in the research already cited here.

**A conclusion he reached alone is not one he argues with.** If we tell him it is legal, that is a
vendor making a claim he has to discount for our motive. If he reads the attorney who argued and won
the case, there is no motive to discount. Same fact, different weight, and we never had to say it
twice. This is Godin's generosity and Cialdini's authority doing the same work from two directions.

**It moves him through the stages without us.** A physician who reads the decision page arrives at
the next email already problem aware, sometimes solution aware. The reading does the stage
progression that would otherwise take three touches.

The rule that follows: **never trim the shelf to shorten the email.** Shorten the body instead. If a
question keeps coming back from physicians, the answer belongs on the shelf permanently, not in the
next email temporarily.

## 5. The mechanics: Cialdini

Seven principles. Four carry weight for us and one is a trap.
([the seven explained](https://www.suebehaviouraldesign.com/en/blog/cialdini-principles-of-persuasion/))

- **Reciprocity.** Must be **given first, unexpected, and personalized.** It *"changes the social
  ledger: now it feels uncomfortable to do nothing in return."* A ruling summary sent to a physician
  who did not ask for it and can genuinely use it is exactly this.
- **Authority.** *"It is evolutionarily sensible to learn from those who already know."* Daniel Siegel
  argued the case and won it. Alice Gosfield is a named health law authority. Their words outperform
  anything we assert about ourselves, and cost us nothing but the citation.
- **Unity.** Shared identity, added in *Pre-Suasion*, and stronger than liking. This is Godin's
  "people like us" in another vocabulary, and it is the level-five answer from §2.
- **Social proof.** Works hardest *"when people question if they're alone."* A physician wondering
  whether this is a fringe arrangement is exactly that person.
- **Scarcity is the trap.** It *"works best when someone is already convinced of the value."* Used
  earlier it *"signals desperation rather than demand."* **We have no deadline and we must never
  invent one.**

## 6. Why a cadence exists at all

Two evidence bases that justify the machine, and correct how we judge it.

**Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow*.** Growth comes from **mental availability**, being the thing he
thinks of at the moment the subject becomes relevant, and from reaching **light buyers**, not from
deepening loyalty among the few already engaged.
([principles](https://www.themarketingstudent.com/how-brands-grow/))

The practical consequence: **a touch that gets no reply is not a wasted touch.** It is not supposed to
convert. It is supposed to be there when a carrier denial or a partner conversation makes the subject
live for him. Judging an individual touch by its reply rate measures the wrong thing.

**Binet and Field, *The Long and the Short of It*.** 996 IPA campaigns, 1980 to 2010. The optimum split
is about **60% long-term brand building, 40% short-term activation.** Activation spikes and dies.
Brand compounds.
([summary](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-peter-field-the-long-and-the-short-of-it))

**Our cadence is close to 100% activation.** Every touch asks for something. There is an open question
here about what the other 60% looks like for a business our size, and it is worth answering rather
than assuming the answer is simply more email.

## 7. Perceived value: Rory Sutherland

*Alchemy.* **Psycho-logic**: value lives in perception, not in the spreadsheet. And **costly
signalling**: *"bits deliver information, but costliness carries meaning."*

Effort is itself a message. A printed brief, a real event, a drive to his office all signal something
an email cannot, precisely **because** they cost us something. The cheapness of email is why volume
alone will never carry a relationship over the line.

## 8. Honest diagnosis of what we send today

Not a criticism of approved copy. A read of it against §1.

**Touch 1 is closer to right than anything else we have.** It opens on the ruling, not on us. It
reframes what he thought he knew. It cites Siegel by name and links his own words. That is Youtility,
authority and unique mechanism in a single message, and it was built right.

**The one live question:** the program paragraph is stage-four content sitting in a stage-one message.
Eric's position is that vague messaging does not work and that it belongs in most emails. That
position is defensible on §2 grounds, because a level-four market wants proof early and we are not in
a virgin market.

This should be **measured, not argued.** We have outbox data. Nothing gets changed on the strength of
a book.

## 9. Checklist

`COPY-RULES.md` first. Then:

- [ ] Which **awareness stage** is this man in, and does the message match it? (§1)
- [ ] Am I writing more than one stage ahead of him?
- [ ] Does it open on **his** world?
- [ ] Is there something genuinely useful here whether or not he ever replies? (§4)
- [ ] Does it beat **doing nothing** this week, from his chair? (§3)
- [ ] Is the proof **named and cited**, not asserted by us? (§5)
- [ ] Short body, and is the shelf behind it deep enough to answer his next question? (§4.1)
- [ ] Did I trim a link to shorten the email? Put it back.
- [ ] No invented scarcity, no deadline we made up
- [ ] Nothing invented: no number, name, legal conclusion or claim about his business
